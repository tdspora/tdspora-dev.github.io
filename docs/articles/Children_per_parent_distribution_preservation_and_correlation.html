FK Sampling Strategy: Without-Replacement Approach

Problem Statement​

When generating synthetic data for tables linked by foreign keys, the inference pipeline samples latent vectors from the trained latent space to produce new rows. The original approach used sampling with replacement (latent_subset.sample(nb_samples, replace=True)).

This creates a distribution problem: some latent vectors get sampled multiple times (producing duplicate parent associations), while others are never selected — even when the original data has a specific children-per-parent distribution. The synthetic child table ends up with a skewed FK frequency that doesn't match the original.

Birthday Paradox Effect​

The issue is a direct manifestation of the birthday paradox. When drawing $k$ samples uniformly at random (with replacement) from a pool of $n$ items:

  • The expected number of unique items selected is:

$$E[\text{unique}] = n \cdot \left(1 - \left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right)^k\right)$$

  • After only $k \approx \sqrt{2n}$ draws, there is a ~50% probability of at least one collision (duplicate selection).

Practical example: With $n = 5{,}000$ parent rows and $k = 5{,}000$ child rows to generate (same-size inference), we expect approximately 3160 unique parents to appear in the sample — meaning ~63% of parents get at least one child, but many get 2–3 while ~37% get none at all.

For the housing_conditions dataset (child of housing_properties), the original data has a specific children-per-parent distribution. Sampling with replacement destroys this distribution by concentrating children on a subset of "lucky" parents while leaving others orphaned entirely.

Solution: Without-Replacement Sampling​

Instead of drawing latent vectors independently at random (with replacement), the new approach shuffles the full latent space and takes consecutive slices from it.

Key difference:

With replacementWithout replacement
SelectionEach draw is independent; same vector can be picked multiple timesEach vector is used exactly once per full pass
Parent coverage~63% of parents appear (birthday paradox)100% of parents appear in every full pass

Analysis​

1. Strategy effect: minimal at sizes ≥ N​

At infer sizes 1×, 2×, and 10×, there is no statistically significant difference between with-replacement and without-replacement sampling on correlation quality (all p > 0.2). Both strategies produce comparable median correlation differences.

The only exception is 0.5× size, where without-replacement performs significantly worse (median 0.0754 vs 0.0087, p = 0.0009). This is expected: when generating fewer rows than the original, without-replacement uses each latent vector at most once, resulting in sparse coverage of the latent space. With-replacement can still revisit high-density regions, maintaining better correlation structure at small sizes.

2. Size effect: dominant factor​

Output size is the primary driver of correlation quality:

  • With-replacement degrades significantly at 2× and 10× (p < 0.001) — forced to resample from a finite latent space produces repetitions that don't add statistical diversity.
  • Without-replacement degrades less sharply at 2× and 10× (p = 0.2, p = 0.09, both NS) — each new pass through the shuffled space provides fresh ordering, resulting in more graceful degradation.

Optimal quality is at size = N (1×) for both strategies, with median correlation diff ~0.01–0.03.

3. CPP distribution: without-replacement is significantly better at large sizes​

At 10× infer size, the difference is stark:

  • With-replacement produces a heavy-tailed CPP distribution (max = 29, mean = 3.0) — some parents accumulate nearly 3× the original maximum children count due to repeated random sampling of the same latent vectors.
  • Without-replacement keeps the distribution tighter (max = 16, mean = 2.8) — each pass through the latent space is complete, preventing extreme concentration on "lucky" parents.

The ratio of synthetic max to original max: 2.9× for with-replacement vs 1.6× for without-replacement.

4. Key trade-off​

CriterionWinner
Correlation quality at size NTie (no significant difference)
Correlation quality at size < NWith-replacement
Correlation quality at size > NWithout-replacement (degrades less)
Theoretical CPP preservationWithout-replacement
Determinism / reproducibilityWithout-replacement (no random duplication)

Conclusion​

Size matters more than strategy for correlation preservation. Both sampling approaches produce equivalent quality at the most common use case (infer size = N).

Recommendation: Use without-replacement as the default because:

  1. It provides equal or better correlation quality at sizes ≥ N
  2. It guarantees full coverage of the latent space per pass (no orphaned parents)
  3. It degrades more gracefully at large infer sizes (2×, 10×)
  4. It is architecturally simpler and more deterministic

Caveat: For infer sizes < N (generating fewer rows than original), with-replacement is preferred since without-replacement loses latent space coverage.
